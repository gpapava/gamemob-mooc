Safety first

  • Do this with an adult, behind a barrier or well back from the kerb. Never step into the road.
  • Do not do it at a crossing. Choose a straight stretch of pavement with a clear view.
  • Do not wave at drivers or try to distract them. Do not point anything that looks like a speed gun.
  • If the school has a risk assessment for outdoor work, follow it. If you cannot go outside, your teacher will give you timing data instead.

Part A. Time ten vehicles

Mark two points exactly 20 m apart along the kerb. One person calls “start” when the front of a vehicle passes the first mark and “stop” at the second; another person times it. Speed in km/h = 72 ÷ seconds (because 20 m in t seconds is 20 ÷ t m/s, and m/s × 3.6 = km/h).

Part B. How far to stop?

Take your fastest measured speed. Use reaction time 1.0 s and friction 0.7 (dry) or 0.4 (wet).
