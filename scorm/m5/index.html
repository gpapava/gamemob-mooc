    What the simulation does
    • One step per second for 600 seconds. Cars arrive at random (14 a minute at the school peak, 5 at midday); pedestrians at 9 a minute at the peak, 1.5 at midday. A green releases one car every 2 seconds.
    • Pedestrians who wait 35 seconds give up and cross on red. That is what really happens, and it is counted separately.
    • Fixed timer: the lights repeat the same cycle whether anyone is there or not. Push button: pedestrians get their phase once the minimum car green is over. Sensor: it also sees the cars, and turns early when the approach is empty; the walk time grows with the number of people waiting.
    • Cars still in the queue at the end count with the wait they have had so far, so nobody can improve the numbers by leaving people waiting.
    • A sensor costs money, needs maintenance and can fail. A good simulation tells you what the technology could do, not whether it is worth buying.
